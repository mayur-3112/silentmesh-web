Our decoy got faster when we made it safer

3 October 2026 · SilentMesh Systems

We published that our Modbus decoy answers in 95.6 ms, beside 100.9 ms on a real PLC. On 3 October we measured it again. The decoy we ship was answering in 24 ms, four times faster than the PLC it imitates. A single timing probe would have told them apart. Nobody had noticed, us included.

Where the 95.6 ms came from

A real PLC answers Modbus once per scan cycle, so its replies sit in a tight band about one cycle wide. Our decoy imitated that with a short sleep: 10 ms plus part of a 20 ms window. That was never enough to reach 100 ms. Most of the 95.6 ms was the decoy writing each request to its evidence database before it replied. The number was real, but the reason was an accident.

How a safety fix broke it

On 30 September we closed the most serious row in our threat model: decoys ran as root and wrote evidence directly. Now they run as an unprivileged user with no capabilities and hand each event to the daemon over a validated pipe. Writing to a pipe takes microseconds. The database write disappeared from the reply path, and the decoy's timing collapsed with it.

Modbus decoy, same harness, 200 requests     median    spread (IQR)
before the safety fix (database write)       72.0 ms   38.2 ms
after the safety fix (event pipe)            24.4 ms   13.1 ms
real OpenPLC, 4 September                   100.9 ms    1.8 ms

Every functional test still passed, because none of them measured time. Our fingerprint probe checks the shape of the timing, that it is not flat, and a fast distribution can still have the right shape.

The fix

The reply now waits until a deadline counted from the moment the request arrived. Whatever logging costs, a pipe or a database or a slow disk, the reply leaves on the modelled schedule: a floor near 94 ms, a per-deployment offset so two installs do not share a signature, part of a short scan window, and the occasional busy cycle.

after the deadline fix (event pipe)         101.3 ms    3.0 ms
after the deadline fix (database write)     101.3 ms    2.9 ms

Same result either way, which is the point. A new test pins the profile and the deadline. We ran it against the old code to make sure it can fail, and it does: a 40 ms log write moved the median from 20.6 to 64.0 ms.

A second check that could not fail

While we were there, we re-ran our probe self-check, which feeds each fingerprint probe a decoy broken on purpose. One probe, the one that checks the decoy rejects addresses outside its map, passed the broken decoy. An earlier change had moved the bounds check, and the mutation that removes it was still matching a different line. It is fixed; all seven scored probes now fail their broken decoys.

What is still open

  • The decoy and the PLC figures were measured on different days. A side-by-side rerun comes next.
  • The spread is 3.0 ms against 1.8 ms: close, not matched.
  • The other decoys have no real device to compare against yet, so their timing is modelled, not measured against hardware.
the rule

A fix for one property can quietly break another. We now treat timing as a tested property, not a side effect, and we publish the number we had to take back next to the one that replaced it.
