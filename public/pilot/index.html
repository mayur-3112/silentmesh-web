pilot

What a pilot involves

30 days · one segment · criteria agreed first

One Linux host on one network segment the plant chooses. Two weeks in MONITOR mode, ideally during commissioning or site acceptance testing: it observes and logs, and changes nothing. Then 30 days with the decoys on. The plant can unplug the host at any time, for any reason. Nothing else on the network depends on it.

Pass criteria

Agreed in writing before anything is connected. Each line can be checked by the plant itself, without relying on our console.

#Pass lineHow the plant checks it
1 no impactZero production stops, alarms or SCADA communication faults caused by SilentMeshThe plant's own alarm log and communication counters, compared with the monitor-only fortnight
2 never initiatesThe host opens zero connections to any PLC, HMI or SCADA machineThe switch or firewall log for the host's IP address
3 fails open, liveHost unplugged and software stopped with the integrator present: SCADA traffic carries on with no faultWatched in the room and written into the acceptance record
4 catches a scanA test scan, on a date the plant picks without telling us, raises a high-priority alert within 60 secondsThe plant's clock against our alert time
5 few false alarmsNo more than one high-priority false alert per week, averaged over 30 daysEvery high alert is listed weekly; the plant marks each one real or false
6 stays upDecoys answer at least 99% of the built-in liveness checksUptime table in the weekly summary
7 leaves no traceFull removal in under 30 minutes on requestDone in front of the plant

Criteria 1 to 3 are the safety gate. If any of them fails, the pilot stops, whatever the others show. Criteria 4 to 7 are the value test. A plant can tighten any line before the start.

What the plant gets

  • A weekly one-page summary: what reached the decoys, each high alert with the plant's real or false marking, and uptime.
  • A pilot report at day 30 and a review meeting.
  • All recorded events stay on site and belong to the plant. Nothing leaves the site unless the plant says so.
  • The pilot fee is credited in full against the first year if the plant continues. A pass does not oblige the plant to buy.

What we need to start

  • Commissioning or acceptance dates. Connecting before go-live makes the host part of the accepted baseline from day one.
  • The integrator's contact. They receive a one-page install note before anything is connected.
  • One segment, one switch port, one IP address. The host is one declared device on the asset register.
  • Alert recipients, and whether anything may leave the site.
  • A named plant contact for the weekly summary.

Host requirements

Linux with kernel 6.6 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04 works as shipped), installed from a Debian package or a container image. No agent on any plant device, no change to any controller, no inline appliance.
